## Research statement

My research training focused on **contact-rich manipulation** when geometric uncertainty exceeds what position control alone can guarantee. Peg-in-hole assembly with tight clearance is a canonical benchmark: it forces you to combine **perception** (where is the hole?), **control** (how hard to push?), and **human-in-the-loop** strategies when autonomy is brittle.

At Innopolis University I explored this problem on a **KUKA LBR iiwa** with ROS, a 3D Systems Touch haptic device, and optional VR interfaces. The thesis compared **four control modes**: full autonomy, bilateral teleoperation with force feedback, shared autonomy, and learning from demonstration — with user studies across scenarios. That work extended an earlier line on **hybrid vision/force control** and deep-learning-based pick, published at IEEE NIR 2021 ([paper](/publication/2021-08-26-robotic-pick-and-assembly/)).

What I took from research into industry is not a single algorithm, but a habit: **instrument the system**, **reproduce failures**, and **treat the boundary between sim, lab, and field as first-class**. Warehouse AMRs, autonomous forklifts, and now AV commissioning all reward the same discipline — only the failure modes and safety bar move.
